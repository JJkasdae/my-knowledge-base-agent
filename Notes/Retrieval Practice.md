# Retrieval Practice

Pulling information out of memory strengthens it more than putting it in again. Trying to recall a fact and getting it wrong leaves you better off than re-reading the correct answer would have.

The mechanism is that recall is the thing being trained. Reading trains recognition, and recognition is not what an exam, or a conversation, or an interview asks for. Every time the answer is retrieved rather than looked at, the path back to it gets easier to find.

Why it feels wrong: recall is effortful and produces visible failure, while re-reading is smooth and produces a feeling of competence. The comfortable method and the effective method are different methods, and the discomfort is the part doing the work.

**Failure condition.** It stops working if the answer is visible while you are trying to retrieve it. Notes open, slides on the second screen, the page half covered by a hand. Then it collapses back into re-reading with extra steps.

Cheapest form: shut the book and write down what was in it. Anything more elaborate than that is optimisation.
