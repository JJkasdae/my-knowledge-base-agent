# Make It Stick

Read this over the winter break because someone in my methods tutorial would not shut up about it. Brown, Roediger and McDaniel, on how learning actually works as opposed to how it feels like it works.

The argument that stuck: the study methods that feel most productive are usually the worst ones. Re-reading a chapter feels like learning because the text gets easier each time, and that easiness is fluency with the page, not knowledge of the material.

Three things I took out of it:

- Testing yourself is not a way of measuring learning, it is a way of causing it. This is the one that changed what I do, and it has its own note now.
- Spacing beats cramming, and the gap is doing the work. Coming back to something after you have half forgotten it is the point, not a failure of scheduling.
- Difficulty during study is a signal you are building something. Difficulty in the exam is a signal you did not.

The bit I am not sure about is how much of the research is on undergraduates being tested on word lists, which is not the same as sitting an essay exam in June.
